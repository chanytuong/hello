# Phase D — external research

Status: COMPLETE · Research date: 2026-10-06 · Next phase: E, final movement/body-language law.

## Scope and evidence

24 sources used: eight firsthand game-development accounts, nine artist/animation teaching sources, four original human-motion papers and three original pixel-animation tutorials. Source evidence and proposed NEVERLOOKBACK rules are separate: the latter are design deductions to test, not findings about those games or scientific prescriptions.

Completed Phase A/B/C evidence was reused. No skills sweep, runtime audit, reference reclassification, gameplay edits or production raster occurred. GDC Vault listings were located, but metadata supplied no animation evidence and is not counted. Embedded videos were not watched; exact source-game exposures, loops and contact windows were not measured. Interviews are primary developer testimony even when hosted by a publication. Relevant scientific excerpts were read; access challenges and indexed-text use are disclosed below.

## Source ledger

### S01 — Dead Cells — Thomas Vasseur, Art Design Deep Dive (2018)

[Original source](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)

Evidence: Convincing timing and minimal keys precede extra drawings; attacks are pose-to-pose. The reported character height is 50 pixels.

### S08 — SANABI — Wonder Potion interview (2023)

### S09 — Stan Prokopenko — How to Draw Gesture

[Original source](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-gesture/)

Read: Instructor lesson notes; not community comments.

Evidence: Gesture connects masses and expresses an action rather than tracing contours; broad action rhythms and contrasting axes organize the figure.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer a whole-body intention before cuffs, folds or flame. Line of action is a drawing abstraction, not a measured center of mass.

### S10 — Stan Prokopenko — Structure in the Body: Robo Bean

[Original source](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-structure-in-the-body-robo-bean)

Read: Instructor lesson notes, landmarks/motion/twist.

Evidence: Separate ribcage and pelvis volumes clarify tilt, lean, twisting and overlapping planes.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer connected torso masses and consistent foreshortening. Boxes are analytical scaffolding, never procedural production body parts.

### S11 — Shawn Kelly — Twinning (2017)

[Original source](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/twinning-and-why-you-should-usually-avoid-it/)

Read: Animator-authored article; posing and timing sections.

Evidence: Matching poses and timing across paired parts can remove contrast and overlap; deliberate symmetry can still communicate an intended action.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer motivated unequal limbs. Do not add random asymmetry or prohibit a brief bilateral protective response.

### S12 — Nathaniel Seymour / Animation Mentor — Overlap and Follow-Through (2025)

[Original source](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-animate-overlap-and-follow-through/)

Read: Written tutorial summary and tips; embedded video not watched.

Evidence: Primary action leads; attached parts trail and settle. Excess overlap floats; negative space must remain clear.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer body-first secondary flame and material response. Exclude Maya controls, graph-editor smoothing and automatic offsets.

### S13 — Kevin Koch — Slow In and Slow Out (2017)

[Original source](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/slow-in-and-slow-out-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/)

Read: Animator-authored article; spacing and alternative stop solutions.

Evidence: Timing and spacing differ. Different parts follow their own paths; abrupt stops, recoil and overshoot can be appropriate alternatives to smooth easing.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer deliberate spacing inside existing durations. Do not tween raster frames or impose cinematic slowdowns.

### S14 — Pedro Medeiros — Thoughts on Low Resolution (2022)

[Original source](https://saint11.art/blog/thoughts_on_low_resolution/)

Read: Artist's complete article text and examples.

Evidence: Meaning comes from selective pixels, meaningful colors and readable masses; details and texture need a reason.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer omission and material hierarchy. A successful small example is not evidence that this protagonist already reads at 1×.

### S15 — Pedro Medeiros — Pixel Art Glossary

[Original source](https://saint11.art/blog/glossary/)

Read: Artist's complete glossary text.

Evidence: Connected same-color pixels form clusters; isolated pixels attract attention, and diagonal connections are weaker than full side connections.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer cluster inspection and justified exceptions. Connectivity statistics cannot prove anatomy or character identity.

### S16 — cure — The Pixel Art Tutorial (2010, edited 2014)

[Original source](https://pixeljoint.com/forum/forum_posts.asp?PID=139318&TID=11299)

### S17 — Stan Prokopenko — How to Draw Interesting Shapes

[Original source](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-interesting-shapes/)

Read: Instructor's public written summary only; video not watched.

Evidence: The summary identifies variety, contrast, complexity, symmetry, tension, balance, shifted peaks and tilted axes as shape-design tools.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer uneven shape hierarchy. Do not attribute detailed procedures or AI-provenance claims to this short summary.

### S18 — Lu et al. — Anticipatory postural adjustments during gait initiation (2017)

[Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5546309/)

Read: Original paper; indexed abstract, introduction/discussion excerpts; direct page challenged access.

Evidence: Self-paced initiation in 157 ambulatory adults showed substantial variability in anticipatory components, including absent components.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer purposeful load transfer without a mandatory universal start sequence. Center of pressure is not center of mass; no lab latency becomes a game delay.

### S19 — Sanders et al. — Protective balance and startle responses to sudden freefall (2015)

[Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4928472/)

Read: Original paper; indexed methods/discussion/conclusion excerpts; direct page challenged access.

Evidence: Nine healthy participants experienced externally triggered versus self-triggered platform drops; first reactions included pronounced neck/arm activity and arm abduction, with habituation.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer a plausible unexpected flinch contrasted with intended action. This is not evidence for a universal fighting stance or for every reaction being protective.

### S20 — Arellano and Kram — Is swinging the arms worth it? (2014)

[Original source](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/217/14/2456/12120/The-metabolic-cost-of-human-running-is-swinging)

Read: Original paper; abstract, methods and rotation/discussion excerpts.

Evidence: Restricting arm swing in runners increased shoulder and pelvis rotation; arm swing helps counter rotational effects of leg motion.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer coupled arm/torso behavior. Distance runners at a laboratory pace do not establish a civilian's panic-running style.

### S21 — Solis-Escalante et al. — Balance perturbations and reactive stepping (2021)

[Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9290492/)

Read: Original paper; abstract, participant/task and discussion excerpts.

Evidence: Eleven young able-bodied participants recovered from translated support surfaces with feet-in-place or stepping responses; responses varied with disturbance.

Transfer / boundary: Transfer visible support restoration after overreach. Do not transplant EEG results, laboratory delays or a physical step into gameplay.

## Civilian motion and research limits

None of these papers observes an untrained person attacking under threat. Desperate running, awkward acceleration, shove, forearm contact and shoulder contact are authored hypotheses derived from connected masses, load transfer and protective/recovery behavior. They require believable attachment and support, rather than an invented scientific claim about aggression. Trained combat demonstrations are not being treated as civilian reference.

Static and moving balance differ. A quiet stance needs plausible support under its mass; a running or reaching mass can travel beyond the current foot if subsequent support catches it. Do not enforce static balance on every running frame. The visible torso center is only a drawing proxy for whole-body mass. Anatomical volume and attachment matter more than numeric pseudo-physics here.

The anti-generic deductions combine S04, S07, S11, S13–S17 with the brief's authored-omission requirements. They are craft criteria, not an AI-image detector or a provenance judgment about the supplied reference. Technically neat work can remain generic when pose intent, hierarchy and transitions are interchangeable.
