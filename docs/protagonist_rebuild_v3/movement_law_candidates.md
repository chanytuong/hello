# Phase D — movement-law candidates

Status: COMPLETE, TEXT ONLY. Final movement/body-language law: NOT SELECTED. Final palette and master poses: NOT STARTED.

## Evaluation method

These are three whole-body alternatives, not a menu of copied poses. Their causal chains differ: upper-body commitment and catch; support-driven protective release; diagonal countertwist and unwind. Each must persist through the same game's quiet, locomotion, contact and recovery behavior. Frame counts, permanent delays, limb-side assignment, exact measurements and hex palette are deliberately uncommitted.

Scores are provisional art-direction judgments (integers 0–10), not measured animation results. A high score does not pass production QA. Body identity, gameplay readability and feasibility must EACH be at least 8; no average can override a failed gate. The other axes expose risks. No candidate is chosen by total.

Evidence constraints: [gesture](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-gesture/), [torso construction](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-structure-in-the-body-robo-bean), [gait variability](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5546309/), [arm/torso coupling](https://journals.biologists.com/jeb/article/217/14/2456/12120/The-metabolic-cost-of-human-running-is-swinging), [support restoration](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9290492/), and the saved game clock constrain these proposals. Specific stress behavior and flame self-image are authored interpretations, not established findings about civilians.

## Candidate A — Reach, then catch up

Disposition: **RETAINED FOR PHASE E COMPARISON**, 86/100 provisional total.

| Required field | Text proposal |
|---|---|
| Movement initiator | The upper chest commits toward the intended action; pelvis and supports visibly catch that commitment. This is a relation among authored keys, never a delayed movement input. |
| Body-part delay | The trailing shoulder and non-grip forearm respond after the chest; pelvis catch precedes final wrist/flame settling. Offsets compress or disappear when a state is too short. |
| Center of gravity / mass | Projected mass approaches the front support and briefly outruns it during exertion; the next actual support restores balance. Quiet stance stays believable rather than permanently pitched. |
| Shoulder rhythm | One shoulder reaches farther and returns later, with restrained counterrotation on the other side. Contact temporarily interrupts the alternating rhythm. |
| Pelvis rhythm | The pelvis follows chest commitment, loads the support and returns under the torso. It has less excursion than the chest and still responds to the alternating legs. |
| Arm rhythm | The action/grip arm reaches with intent; the other participates unevenly as a counterweight or protective check. No rigid rear arm, twin elbows or automatic mirrored half-cycle. |
| Leg rhythm | A complete alternating cycle with brisk recovery to catch the forward mass, traceable knees and feet, and distinct load versus airborne moments. Unequal performance must still have credible supports. |
| Idle identity | A small unfinished inclination to act, checked by the pelvis/support before it becomes a stride; one elbow settles later. Restraint makes the next commitment noticeable. |
| Locomotion identity | The person appears slightly ahead of their own balance: hurried support recovery follows torso commitment. Desperation comes from timing and restoration, not a trained sprint wedge. |
| Attack identity | A too-eager connected reach or bodily shove, with supported forward contact and recognizable grip. No expert guard, long punch windup or new strike type. |
| Recovery identity | The action overshoots its comfortable reach, then the support and pelvis retrieve the torso; the returning arm follows. Confirmed contact rebounds; a whiff exposes the overreach. |
| Gameplay risk | A leading chest can falsely suggest early hit reach or movement startup. Keep root and damage windows authoritative; body excursion must not promise contact before active time. |
| Animation risk | At native size the chase of chest/pelvis can become constant falling, shirt drift or foot slide. Needs conservative volume, clear support changes and actual transition testing. |
| Genericness risk | A constant forward lean would reduce this to the familiar action runner. The defining behavior is commitment followed by a visible catch across idle/run/contact/recovery, with quiet counterposes. |

Score rationale: commitment/catch offers strong state-spanning identity and an exaggerated flame/body disagreement. Readability and feasibility are only 8 because leading reach and distance-sampled foot contact may miscue action. Mirroring is comparatively simple if grip roles stay consistent.

## Candidate B — Brace, then drive

Disposition: **RETAINED FOR PHASE E COMPARISON**, 86/100 provisional total.

| Required field | Text proposal |
|---|---|
| Movement initiator | Support loading and pelvis drive initiate exertion; the ribcage initially protects itself, then opens into the action. These drawings track existing acceleration and contact rather than adding a pause. |
| Body-part delay | Chest opening trails pelvic drive; the free elbow loosens after the shoulder, while the grip wrist remains connected. At contact, protective recoil precedes loose secondary settling. |
| Center of gravity / mass | Mass stays comparatively near the loaded support and travels forward in short commitment; the body quickly recontains it. Avoid an implausible perfectly centered mass in every running frame. |
| Shoulder rhythm | One shoulder remains higher/nearer the neck during preparation while the other opens into motion, then both release unequally. This must read as protection, not a combat guard. |
| Pelvis rhythm | Support alternation leads restrained pelvic tilt/turn; ribcage turn follows at a different amplitude. The pelvis provides a readable base for the short body drive. |
| Arm rhythm | One arm remains compact while the other helps balance or maintains the grip. Neither is frozen; small unequal releases prevent a robot or held boxing guard. |
| Leg rhythm | Full alternating contacts with efficient passing and a perceptible loaded/down phase. A support-driven rhythm permits an awkward first push without a distinct slow walk mechanic. |
| Idle identity | Uneven protective enclosure around the torso with a small release of tension rather than full symmetrical breathing. Weight rests on one credible support without a heroic wide stance. |
| Locomotion identity | A contained, urgent run: legs drive and the protected chest reluctantly opens. Stress is conveyed by constrained arm use and torso compensation rather than luxurious stride extension. |
| Attack identity | A short supported bodily drive into forearm/shove-like contact, followed by involuntary reclosure. The flame can exaggerate confidence while the physical body remains apprehensive. |
| Recovery identity | The chest closes and mass returns to support before elbows and clothing settle. Contact recoil differs from an empty shove while preserving the same protective person. |
| Gameplay risk | A brace can look like slow startup or a blocking ability. Show it within current clocks, without waiting, shield cues, reduced damage or control lock. |
| Animation risk | Compact arms and dark torso can merge at 1×; an overly low pelvis becomes constant crouching. Requires a strong elbow gap, visible wrist/grip and alternating leg ownership. |
| Genericness risk | Protective posture can drift into armored ninja, fighter guard or old stock hunch. Keep civilian apprehension and release, body-driven gait, modest stance and consequential recovery. |

Score rationale: support and compact contact fit the short clocks with fewer occlusion changes. Readability and feasibility look stronger in text; identity and distinctiveness only reach 8 because protective enclosure can become a generic hunch. The flame may represent confidence that the physical body does not share.

## Candidate C — Twist, then unwind

Disposition: **REJECTED**, 80/100 provisional total.

| Required field | Text proposal |
|---|---|
| Movement initiator | A diagonal ribcage twist stores apparent intent against a briefly resisting pelvis; the body unwinds toward the action. No rotational delay may alter actual movement or attack startup. |
| Body-part delay | Pelvis and the trailing shoulder resolve after the ribcage turn; arms take unequal crossing paths and release later. This creates more overlap relationships than the short clocks may support. |
| Center of gravity / mass | Mass moves diagonally across support before projecting forward; unwind can leave it off center until the returning foot/body catches it. Static and airborne requirements must remain distinct. |
| Shoulder rhythm | Pronounced unequal forward/back shoulder motion, with changes in which plane is exposed. Compression and release would have to remain readable after horizontal mirroring. |
| Pelvis rhythm | Counterturn rather than matching shoulder turn, followed by a smaller delayed unwind into the new support. Waist connection is essential, not a swiveling cutout joint. |
| Arm rhythm | One arm initially folds across the torso while the other trails, then they separate along unequal arcs. Grip ownership must remain anatomical even when the arms overlap. |
| Leg rhythm | Alternating support carries the torso's diagonal action, with uneven passing accents and a corrective gather. No lateral gameplay step or new stance transition is permitted. |
| Idle identity | A residual uneven twist held between chest and pelvis, released by a small support adjustment. Avoid an unexplained perpetual snake-like sway. |
| Locomotion identity | Urgent running with a restrained winding/unwinding torso accent across contacts. Its rhythm is distinct on paper but risks concealing travel direction and leg phase. |
| Attack identity | Awkward diagonal unwind into existing forward contact, followed by an imbalanced return. A rotational flourish must not suggest a spin attack, martial skill or broader hitbox. |
| Recovery identity | Counterturn checks the initial unwind, then support and limbs gather. Excess reversal could consume the entire 80 ms recovery and leave no readable restored key. |
| Gameplay risk | Changing visible torso planes can miscue facing, reach and contact during a short startup. Crossed arms can conceal the existing held weapon and direction. |
| Animation risk | Frequent occlusion swaps, foreshortening and mirror constraints are difficult to author consistently at roughly 56 px body height; no native proof exists to support feasibility. |
| Genericness risk | Strong shape contrast can still become a stock stylish fighter or serpentine motion gimmick. A habitual twist is not by itself a civilian identity. |

Rejection rationale: readability 7 and feasibility 7 fail mandatory thresholds despite identity 9. Low-res silhouette 7 and mirror compatibility 6 identify why: twisting across planes creates ownership and weapon-overlap changes faster than the current short contact/recovery clock can comfortably show. Flame exaggeration cannot compensate for these failures. Do not progress this candidate into production under this assessment.

## Open decision and disproof conditions

A and B remain competing directions. Equal totals do not imply equivalence or selection; their risk profiles differ. E must judge whether commitment/catch or protective drive better serves the actual character, then define one coherent law and exact construction/palette. Do not blend all three into arbitrary limb delays.

Both retained candidates still need the later eight-master proof and real animation: body-only identity at 1×; both facings with the same anatomical grip; supported contacts; state transitions at actual timing; stable cloth and limb volumes; flame as attached self-image rather than rescue. Any failure can lower a mandatory score and reject a retained direction. Candidate C is rejected now; reconsideration would require materially changing the proposal and rescoring, not ignoring its gate.

No forced corrective footstep, movement pause, guard mechanic, wider hitbox, slower jump or new attack is permitted. No art has been drawn to validate these hypotheses. Real stressed-civilian motion evidence, native-size silhouette results, mirror grip quality, foot-slide measurements and interruption behavior remain unresolved.
